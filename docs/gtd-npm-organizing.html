Natural Planning: organizing

Work in progress.

Turn available project ideas into just enough structure to see useful next work. Start here when project ideas exist but their relationships, sequence or priorities are unclear. Earlier stages are useful context, not prerequisites you must repeat.

Structure should reveal a next move, not manufacture certainty.

Useful terms

A component groups related ideas or work. A dependency must be satisfied before another step proceeds; a sequence can be required or merely preferred.

A question to start with

Which piece depends on another being finished first?

Try it together

  1. Read existing ideas and the intended outcome. Group related components without losing source or selection status.
    Look for: A comprehensible set of themes or components exists.
  2. Identify dependencies, ordering and relative importance against the agreed purpose and outcome. Label uncertain relationships.
    Look for: The outline distinguishes prerequisites from independent work.
  3. Separate selected components, alternatives and evidence gaps. Choose the smallest useful outline or map rather than inventing a complete schedule.
    Look for: The user can see what could move next and what remains undecided.

A fictional example

Workshop ideas become venue, materials and instruction components. Capacity depends on a verified venue; a draft lesson can progress independently. An outdoor option remains unassessed, not silently rejected.

Avoid this trap

This is project planning, not the GTD inbox-organizing workflow. Avoid invented estimates, owners and deadlines; a group of ideas is not an approved plan.

Pause or continue

You can stop with a useful output. To return later, ask your partner to keep an agreed checkpoint in your private work workspace, including what is settled, what is still a proposal and the next question. File naming and handoff conventions are Working Partner features, not GTD requirements.

Possible next step: actions. Continue only if it helps; you can start at any stage.
