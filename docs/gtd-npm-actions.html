Natural Planning: next actions

Work in progress.

Identify currently feasible, visible actions and keep blocked future steps separate. Start here when a project needs a concrete next move rather than more general planning. Earlier stages are useful context, not prerequisites you must repeat.

Ready work, proposed ownership and accepted assignments are different facts.

Useful terms

A next action is a concrete activity with an observable output. A planning action is specific thinking needed before execution.

A question to start with

What can move now, and who has actually agreed to move it?

Try it together

  1. Inspect the current outcome, outline and actual prerequisites. Identify which parts can move now.
    Look for: Ready work is separated from blocked or speculative future work.
  2. Express each useful next move as a concrete activity with a recognizable result. Identify a proposed actor only when supported.
    Look for: The action can be started without repeating the planning decision.
  3. If the gap is planning or evidence, name that activity specifically. Confirm what the user wants to do, delegate or leave as a proposal.
    Look for: A small actionable set is distinguished from assignments and completed work.

A fictional example

“Plan workshop” becomes “Draft three safety points for the practice exercise.” “Book venue” remains blocked until availability, costs and authority are checked.

Avoid this trap

Identifying a next action does not authorize execution, scheduling or contacting anyone. Multiple independent actions may be ready; dependent future steps stay in project support.

Pause or continue

You can stop with a useful output. To return later, ask your partner to keep an agreed checkpoint in your private work workspace, including what is settled, what is still a proposal and the next question. File naming and handoff conventions are Working Partner features, not GTD requirements.

You can now choose whether to act, keep the proposal, or revisit an unclear earlier step. Planning does not itself send, book, assign or commit anything.
