Natural Planning: purpose and principles

Work in progress.

Clarify why this undertaking matters and what must remain true about how it is done. Start here when the project’s reason or guiding standards are unclear. Earlier stages are useful context, not prerequisites you must repeat.

The reason guides direction; principles guide acceptable ways forward.

Useful terms

Purpose is the reason for this project. Principles are standards and boundaries for project choices.

A question to start with

What would make this project worth doing well?

Try it together

  1. Identify the project reason from the user’s actual context. Ask whose need it serves and why it is worth doing.
    Look for: A short purpose statement is grounded in the user’s intent.
  2. Surface guiding principles, constraints and unacceptable trade-offs. Distinguish agreed standards from tentative or disputed proposals.
    Look for: Each material principle has a source and agreement status.
  3. Check the purpose and principles against a real planning choice. Preserve disagreements for the appropriate person rather than deciding their values.
    Look for: The user has a usable decision frame, with unresolved choices visible.

A fictional example

A community workshop aims to let beginners practise a repair safely. Affordable participation and accessible space are proposed principles; the organiser must confirm trade-offs.

Avoid this trap

Project purpose is not a life-purpose exercise. Do not supply the agent’s values or infer agreement from silence.

Pause or continue

You can stop with a useful output. To return later, ask your partner to keep an agreed checkpoint in your private work workspace, including what is settled, what is still a proposal and the next question. File naming and handoff conventions are Working Partner features, not GTD requirements.

Possible next step: outcomes. Continue only if it helps; you can start at any stage.
